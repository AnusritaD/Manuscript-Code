# Manuscript-Code

This repository contains the Python scripts (written for Google Colab) used to generate the figures in the manuscript, based on the survey data collected from the academic institutions ("All_in_one_corrected.csv"). Each script loads the survey data, aggregates responses at the institute level (collapsing multiple respondents per institute where applicable), and produces a visual representation of all the data (saved as high-resolution '.tif' and preview '.png' and '.pdf' files). 

Figure 2.a) Institutions offering relevant life-sciences courses.
Two-panel figure showing (left) the proportion of institutions that do/do not offer courses related to evolution, ecology, or wildlife genetics, and (right) a breakdown of the specific course categories offered.  

Figure 2.b) Major course areas. 
An UpSet plot showing which combinations of genomics-related topics (e.g., Bioinformatics, Phylogenomics, NGS technologies, Genome assembly) are covered together in institutes' curricula, along with per-topic totals. 

Figure 2.c) Integration of genetics/heredity vs. genomics/big-data approaches.
A grouped bar chart comparing how extensively genetics & heredity concepts versus big data & genomics concepts are integrated into evolutionary biology courses, across four levels of integration. 

Figure 2.d) Academic level at which relevant courses are offered.  
A 3-set Venn diagram (paired with a bar chart) showing the overlap between institutes offering courses at the Bachelor's, Master's, and Diploma/Certificate levels. 

Figure 2.e) Availability of hands-on training. 
A horizontal bar chart of the key lab tools, instruments, and setups (e.g., PCR & molecular biology equipment, sequencing tools, bioinformatics resources) available at institutes for evolutionary, ecological, or wildlife-genetics training. 

Figure 2.f) Awareness of freely available online learning resources. 
A stacked figure showing (top) institute-level awareness of free online evolutionary-biology learning modules, and (bottom) an UpSet plot of which specific platforms (e.g., YouTube, Coursera, iBiology, SWAYAM, NPTEL) institutes are aware of. 

Figure 3.a) Major institutional constraints. 
A bar chart of the unique-institute counts for each reported challenge - including infrastructure, funding, skilled personnel, and field-station/data access limitations. 

Figure 3.b) Priorities for improving genomics education and research. 
A bar chart ranking the resources institutes say would most help them teach evolutionary/wildlife genetics - e.g., funding, faculty training, teaching materials, computational infrastructure, and collaborations.
